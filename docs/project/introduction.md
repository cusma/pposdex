# Introduction

Blockchain technology arose to fulfill the promise of creating and exchanging
digital native value in a disintermediated, censorship-resistant, secure and
scalable way.

Billions of people on Earth (and maybe on the Moon or Mars) could transact
without borders, barriers or discrimination, on a distributed computational
infrastructure that removes the need for trusted parties in between.

Just as the exchange of _digital information_ in the Internet era relies on
_Communication Protocols_, the exchange of _digital value_ relies on _Consensus
Protocols_. Combining distributed computation, cryptography and game theory into
a mathematical equilibrium, _Consensus Protocols_ are the engines that power
blockchains, keeping the history of digital value unique, consistent, trustless
and tamper-proof on a distributed public ledger.

A blockchain's performance depends on its Consensus Protocol. At the beginning
of this technological journey, the _Proof of Work_ (PoW) consensus powered the
first generation of blockchains, with the historic merit of proving that
digital native value can exist.

PoW secures the ledger by making validation costly on purpose: miners compete
to validate the next block of data, committing computational and energy
resources to the system, as the name "Proof of Work" suggests. This cost is
what makes rewriting the history so expensive, and it is a deliberate design
choice. The same design, however, bounds how many transactions can be
finalized, how fast, and with how much energy: scaling PoW blockchains has
proven hard, and mining has tended to concentrate in large, specialized
facilities.

The ultimate output of a blockchain infrastructure is reaching consensus to
finalize the _state transitions_ of a distributed permissionless system in a
secure, scalable and decentralized way.

The history of engineering teaches us that _any_ technology evolves towards
higher efficiency and scalability: whoever obtains the _same_ useful output with
fewer resources and less time survives.

Is PoW the only way to reach consensus and securely finalize the state
transitions of a distributed permissionless system?
